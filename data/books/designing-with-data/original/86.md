# Competing metrics
In some cases, you might find that you have two teams that are working on metrics that feel like they are competing with each other. Katie Dill shared a story with us from Airbnb about how they handled a similar situation:


In Katie’s example, you can see the importance of balancing metrics to deliver the best experience (e.g., host quality and host quantity have to work together). This is a good reminder for how it’s important to find the right metric(s) of interest for the goal you’re trying to achieve and how much those metrics might affect the behavior and incentive of the team to design the experience that can deliver those goals.


We fundamentally believe that successful businesses are built on successful user experiences. In other words, the best metrics will align well with both your users’ needs and your business goals. Customers won’t sign up for your service unless they believe that it has value for them. Customers won’t stay with your service or use your products if they are too difficult to use or don’t do what your customers want them to do. As you start to define how you will approach your goals, always ask yourself why you expect those approaches or solutions to positively impact your key business and experience metrics. And as you continue to learn more about your users and business through the data you get back from experiments, keep your eyes open for new metrics you could derive that are important to your business. This is where data, design principles, and business come together.
