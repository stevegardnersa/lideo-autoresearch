# Questions

The book begins by arguing that experiments fail early when the question is vague. A useful question names the decision it is meant to improve, the behavior it is expected to change, and the mechanism that supposedly links the intervention to the outcome.

## Decision linkage

The chapter says every experiment should answer a downstream decision. If no decision changes when the result arrives, the project is probably collecting trivia rather than evidence. This does not mean exploratory work is useless. It means exploratory work should be labeled honestly and judged by whether it generates sharper later questions.

## Mechanism before metric

The author also insists on mechanism before metric. Teams often choose a measurable output first and then invent a story around it. That order is backwards. When the mechanism is explicit, the team can tell whether the chosen metric is central, merely convenient, or badly misaligned with the real hypothesis.
