# Measures

The chapter on measurement explains that metrics are not neutral windows onto reality. Every measure selects one slice of the phenomenon and therefore creates blind spots.

## Good measure design

A good measure is relevant, sensitive enough to move when the underlying process changes, and stable enough that noise does not overwhelm the signal. For example, a weekly conversion rate may be more decision-useful than raw daily counts if traffic varies sharply by day.

## Common trap

A common trap is choosing a metric that is easy to collect but weakly connected to the real objective. Page views can rise while comprehension falls. Time on page can increase because the material is engaging, but it can also increase because the interface is confusing. The metric is not wrong; it is incomplete, and the summary judgment becomes wrong if that incompleteness is ignored.
