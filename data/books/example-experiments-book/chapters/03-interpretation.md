# Interpretation

Results do not interpret themselves. The final chapter argues that good interpretation compares the observed result with the pre-registered expectation, the measurement process, and plausible alternative explanations.

## Alternative explanations

A result may be real yet still fail to support the intended story. Suppose an intervention changes behavior by making the task slower rather than more valuable. If the team only records the primary metric, they may celebrate the improvement while misunderstanding the causal path that produced it.

## Practical rule

The practical rule is to preserve uncertainty instead of hiding it. If two explanations remain plausible, the team should say so and design a follow-up test. This approach can feel slower, but it prevents the organization from building strong strategy on top of weak interpretation.
