# Method

The method chapter turns the framework into a repeatable process. It argues that good summaries and decisions emerge from a short sequence of moves: collect the main claims, identify the causal logic, test the limits of each claim, and only then compress the material.

## Sequence

Step 1 is extraction. The reader lists the chapter's explicit concepts, named distinctions, numbers, and examples. Step 2 is relation mapping. The reader asks how the concepts connect, which claims are foundational, and which claims merely illustrate the larger pattern. Step 3 is pressure testing. The reader searches for conditions under which the claim weakens, reverses, or stops applying.

This process works because compression after analysis is safer than compression before analysis. If the writer shortens too early, they often keep vivid phrases while dropping the mechanism that made the phrase meaningful. For instance, a memorable anecdote may survive while the general principle disappears.

## Output standard

The chapter says the final output should be dense but not cryptic. It should preserve technical terms when they carry meaning, yet it should gloss them once if a new reader may not know them. It should also prefer one representative example over three repetitive examples, unless the variation across examples is itself important.
