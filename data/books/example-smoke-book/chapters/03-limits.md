# Limits

The framework has limits, and the chapter insists that users state them clearly. A structured process can reduce confusion, but it cannot remove disagreement when people have different values, incentives, or risk tolerances.

## Common failure modes

One failure mode is false precision. A neat diagram can make weak evidence look stronger than it is. Another is summary drift, where repeated compression strips away qualifiers until a tentative statement sounds universal. A third is category error: readers sometimes apply a framework built for diagnosis to a context that requires negotiation or experimentation instead.

## Practical caution

The author offers a practical caution. The method is most reliable when the subject has enough stability for concepts to transfer across cases. It is less reliable when the environment changes quickly, because the relationships that explained yesterday's result may not explain tomorrow's. However, this does not make the framework useless. It means the user should update more frequently and treat conclusions as provisional rather than final.
