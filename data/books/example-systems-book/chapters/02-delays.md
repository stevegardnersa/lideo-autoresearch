# Delays

Delays complicate systems because causes and effects are separated in time. When feedback is delayed, decision makers often overreact to the latest signal and create oscillation instead of control.

## Why delays mislead

A delayed system can make a good policy look ineffective in the short run. Suppose inventory orders are increased today, but shipments will not arrive for 6 weeks. If managers judge the decision after only a few days, they may conclude that nothing changed and issue a second, unnecessary correction.

## Practical implication

The author argues that delay awareness changes how performance should be read. It is not enough to ask whether a policy worked; the analyst must also ask when the system could reasonably reveal that it worked. In volatile settings, the combination of strong feedback and long delays is especially dangerous because it rewards urgency while punishing patience.
