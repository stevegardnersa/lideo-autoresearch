# Leverage

Leverage points are places where a relatively small change can produce a large shift in system behavior. The chapter cautions that leverage is not the same thing as visible effort. Highly visible interventions may be politically satisfying while doing little to alter the underlying structure.

## Finding leverage

Leverage usually sits near goals, constraints, rules, and information flows. Changing a report that arrives every month may do less than changing the incentive that determines what the report is used for. Likewise, adding more capacity may help less than redesigning the queue rule that causes congestion in the first place.

## Limits

The chapter includes an important caveat. A leverage point in one system may be irrelevant in another system that looks superficially similar. Analysts therefore need a structural explanation for why a proposed intervention should work, not just a success story from a different context.
